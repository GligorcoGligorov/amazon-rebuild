### The sharpest findings

**Amazon has no responsive site.** It UA-switches. A desktop browser at a 375px viewport gets the desktop template with *no viewport meta tag* — 1000px of content in a 375px window. The real mobile site is a separate template. I had to drive an iPhone 13 UA via a local Playwright script to see it. For our rebuild this is a non-issue but worth knowing: don't copy their desktop CSS and expect it to shrink.

**The mobile cart is the best thing on the site, and it's the opposite of the desktop one.** Mobile puts subtotal + "your order qualifies for FREE delivery" + a full-width `Proceed to checkout (2 items)` *above* the line items. Everything you need to decide is in the first viewport. Steal this exactly.

**The add-to-cart confirmation is a full page interstitial, not a drawer** — and on mobile it's actively worse than desktop: it offers only "Go to Cart", no checkout button, then hands you a sponsored carousel and a credit-card ad. Desktop at least gives you "Proceed to checkout". This is the clearest place where we can beat them cheaply.

**Product pages are enormous.** The same PDP is 10,787px on desktop and 16,176px on mobile. On mobile the sponsored ad sits *above* the title and the price is below the fold, behind a full-viewport product image.

**Sponsored density:** 12 of 60 result cards on desktop, 6 of 26 on mobile — plus banner ads above results, a sponsored item in the PDP right rail, and two ad carousels on the cart confirmation. I also hit genuine duplicates: the same ASIN listed twice in one result page (once sponsored, once organic), and two near-identical adidas listings at $148.50 and $149.95.

**Pagination caps at 7 pages** despite "over 30,000 results" — so roughly 336 of 30,000 items are reachable.
